WISE Financial Literacy

Preface

Why Financial Literacy Matters

Across the United States and within our local communities, young people are navigating increasingly complex financial realities. Access to high-quality financial education is no longer optional—it is essential.

BEAC developed the WISE Financial Literacy curriculum to empower students, particularly those from underserved and urban communities, with the knowledge, confidence, and strategies needed to make informed financial decisions that impact their futures.

Grounded in cultural responsiveness and aligned to Illinois State Financial Literacy Standards, this curriculum provides students with real-world tools for budgeting, saving, earning, and building wealth over time.

WISE—Wealth, Income, Saving, Empowerment—reflects our belief that when we equip young people with financial knowledge, teach practical skills, and nurture their capacity to build wealth, we create lasting generational change.

Through intentional instruction, family engagement, and community partnerships, we aim to ensure every student is not only financially literate but financially powerful.

Welcome to a journey of empowerment, opportunity, and success.

Implementation

Delivery Models

BEAC designed this curriculum with flexibility in mind. Choose the model that best fits your school or program:

Model 1: After-School Programming

Best For: Enrichment programs, community centers, 21st Century Learning Centers.

Format: 1–2 lessons per week over 12–15 weeks. Project-based delivery. Room for guest speakers and field trips. Pacing: 45–60 minutes per session.

Model 2: Homeroom/Advisory Integration

Best For: Schools with daily or weekly advisory/homeroom blocks.

Format: 1 full lesson per week or split across 3–4 sessions. Pacing: 15–25 minute segments. Consistent timing supports habit-building.

Model 3: Elective Course or Mini-Unit

Best For: Standalone units within social studies, CTE, or consumer education courses.

Format: Semester-based or 9-week rotation. Pacing: 2–3 lessons per week. Include end-of-unit projects.

Parent & Community Engagement

Financial education is most powerful when it extends into students' homes and communities. BEAC provides multiple tools to engage families and community partners:

Engagement Strategies

  • Parent Flyers: Every lesson includes a take-home flyer with: what students are learning, discussion prompts for families, and simple at-home activities.
  • Family Financial Literacy Nights: Host showcases during PTA meetings, back-to-school night, or student-led conferences.
  • Guest Panels: Invite local professionals (bankers, entrepreneurs, alumni) to share their financial journeys.
  • Social Media: Encourage students to share financial tips via school social media or newsletters.

Differentiation

Middle School vs. High School Distinctions

Instructional approaches should be tailored to developmental stages:

Grades 7–8

Focus:

  • Mindset, goal-setting, and basic budgeting.
  • Games, visuals, and scenarios (school store).
  • Journaling guides critical thinking.

Instructional Tips:

Relate to sneakers and lunch money. Use role-playing and interactive movement.

Grades 9–12

Focus:

  • Complex topics: interest, inflation, credit.
  • Career path planning and apps.
  • Independent spending plans.

Instructional Tips:

Connect to college costs and apartments. Emphasize long-term decision sequences.

Best Practices

Instructional Strategies

BEAC recommends the following evidence-based approaches:

✅ Make Content Culturally Responsive

Use examples that reflect lived experiences. Address systemic barriers to wealth-building and celebrate diverse financial traditions.

✅ Frequent Reflection & Journaling

Personal connection deepens learning. It builds self-awareness around existing money beliefs and community-driven norms.

✅ Reinforce with Repetition

Revisit SMART goals, saving strategies, and budgeting principles across lessons to ensure mastery.

✅ Blend Instruction with Active Learning

Mix teacher-led content with discussion, role-play, and peer teaching to increase long-term retention.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can this be taught by non-experts?

Yes! WISE is designed for educators and youth workers. Facilitator Guides include scripts and background info to support any delivery model.

Q: What if our students don't have bank accounts?

The curriculum uses hypothetical scenarios to teach principles. Students learn the logic for their future financial potential.

Q: Can I adapt the pacing?

Absolutely! WISE is designed for flexibility. You can teach 1 lesson per week or condense the content into intensive segments.

Q: How do I handle sensitive topics?

Approach discussions with empathy. Acknowledge systemic barriers and focus on asset-based empowerment rather than deficit narratives.
